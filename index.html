Fetching, cross-checking and scanning today's news for AI-generated content…
The first scan can take a few minutes. Later refreshes only check new articles.

How stories are checked

  1. Every hour the app reads the public news feeds of established news organisations (listed below). Only articles from the last 36 hours are used.
  2. Articles describing the same event are grouped by comparing the words in their headlines and summaries.
  3. A story is only shown if at least two independent organisations reported it. Feeds from the same organisation (BBC UK and BBC World, for example) count once, and titles with the same owner (such as the Reach plc local papers) also count once, because they often share copy.
  4. Where outlets give different death tolls in their headlines, the story is flagged so you can compare them.

AI-generated content check

Before stories are matched, every report that could appear here is checked for signs that it was written by AI rather than a journalist:

  • Publisher labels: some publishers mark AI or automated content in the page itself, or add a note such as "This article was produced with the help of AI". A labelled article is always excluded.
  • Writing patterns: stock AI phrasing, AI-style bylines and disclosure-like sentences are picked out.
  • Claude review (switched off, paid): when switched on in settings.env, Claude reads each article's byline, labels and text, and judges whether the article itself was AI-generated. Writing about AI doesn't count. Articles it rates likely AI-generated with 80% confidence or more are excluded. Articles with possible signs stay in but lose 10 points from their story's score.

Excluded articles are removed before stories are matched, so a story can lose its second source and drop off the page. You're told each time: in the bar at the top of this page, with a Mac notification, and in ai_exclusions_log.jsonl. No AI detector is perfect, and the evidence for each decision is shown so you can judge it yourself.

Authenticity score

Each story gets a percentage that sums up how well it is backed up. It is never 100%. Open "Why this score" on any story to see the breakdown.

  • Independent outlets confirming (up to 72 points): 2 outlets give 45, rising to 72 for 6 or more.
  • Standing of those outlets (up to 15): public-service broadcasters and papers of record score highest, regional commercial titles lowest.
  • How closely the reports agree (up to 10): reports that describe the same facts in similar terms score higher.
  • International spread (up to 5): reported by newsrooms in three or more countries.
  • Conflicting figures (minus 10): outlets give different death tolls.
75–98% well corroborated 60–74% corroborated, check the detail Below 60% thinly sourced, treat with caution

Limits: this is automatic cross-referencing, not human fact-checking. Two outlets agreeing makes a report more likely to be accurate, not certain. Occasionally unrelated articles get grouped, or one story is split into two. Always open the sources to read the detail. Reuters and AP don't offer public feeds, so they are not included directly.
